Prisoner (🧗‍♂️ / 🏃‍♂️) Bound Electrons ($e^-$) Subatomic particles trapped inside the potential well of the metal lattice structure. In this simulation, prisoners face right toward the collector anode.

Deep Cell (-8.0 eV) 		Work Function ($\Phi$) & Binding Energy Levels Work Function ($\Phi = 1.0\text{ eV}$): Minimum energy required to liberate an electron from the outermost surface. Electrons in deeper energy levels require greater energy ($4.0\text{ eV}$ or $8.0\text{ eV}$) due to internal lattice scattering, generating a continuous kinetic energy spectrum ($0 \le K \le K_{\max}$).
High-Altitude Plane (✈️) Incident Light Source The radiation source positioned above the metal surface, delivering energy packets (photons) targeted at specific atomic depths.
Rescue Pack Batch (📦) Photons ($E = hf$) & Energy Quantization Light energy is quantized into discrete packets. Each single pack collides 1-on-1 with strictly one prisoner. Packs drop side-by-side, releasing a vertical column of escaping photoelectrons.
Supply Level / Pack Energy ($1\text{ eV} - 10\text{ eV}$) Light Frequency ($f$) Determines individual photon energy $E = hf$. Photon energy must equal or exceed binding magnitude ($E \ge |\Phi|$) for photoelectron emission to occur; surplus energy converts into kinetic energy.
Drop Rate / Batch Quantity ($1 - 5\text{ packs/s}$) Light Intensity (Photon Flux) Number of incident photons per second. Increasing drop rate raises the photoelectron release count and photocurrent ($I \propto N_e$), but does not increase individual particle stamina/speed.
Prisoner Speed & Stamina Kinetic Energy ($K = E - |\Phi|$) Remaining energy of freed electrons: $K_0 = E - |\Phi|$. For surface electrons, this represents the maximum kinetic energy $K_{\max} = hf - \Phi$. If $K_0 = 0\text{ eV}$, photoelectrons emerge briefly but lack velocity to reach the anode unless accelerated by $+V$.
Guards (👮‍♂️ / $-V$) Retarding Voltage / Reverse Bias Opposing electric field decelerating electrons. If $E - |\Phi| - |V_{\text{ret}}| < 0$, photoelectrons lose all kinetic energy before reaching the collector, turn around facing left, and retreat into the prison cell. The voltage reducing photocurrent to zero is the Stopping Potential ($V_s$) where $eV_s = K_{\max}$.
Attractors (💃 / $+V$) Accelerating Voltage / Forward Bias Forward electric field pulling emitted photoelectrons toward the anode, raising photocurrent until reaching the Saturation Current.
Escape Endpoint (🎯) Anode / Collector Terminal The receiving terminal that collects emitted photoelectrons to close the external circuit.
Average Escape Rate ($\text{people/s}$) Photocurrent ($I_{\text{photo}}$) Rate of photoelectrons successfully reaching the anode per unit time ($I_{\text{photo}} = \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta t}$, displayed in $\mu\text{A}$).
